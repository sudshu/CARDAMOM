How temperature drives respiration in DALEC_1100

A plant respires (burns carbon) to stay alive; soil microbes respire while eating dead material. Both speed up when it gets warmer. DALEC_1100 describes that speed-up with four separate "Q10" dials, each fitted from data. This page shows what a Q10 is, why four are needed, and how they behave over a synthetic year.

1. What is a Q10?

A Q10 answers one question: if it gets 10 °C warmer, how many times faster does this process run? Q10 = 2 means "twice as fast per 10 °C". The model writes it as a multiplier on the base rate:

fT = Q10(T − 25 °C) / 10

At 25 °C the multiplier is exactly 1. Above 25 °C it grows, below it shrinks. Drag the dials: every respiration flux in DALEC_1100 has its own Q10 and its own prior range of 1–5.

2. Why four dials, not one?

Because the four fluxes see different temperatures and are limited by different things:

4. The maintenance-respiration cap, in one picture

The Q10 sets the demand. What the plant can actually pay is R = Rpot·(1 − e−S/Rpot), where S = NSC + GPP − Rd is the day's carbon supply. When supply is generous (S ≫ Rpot) you get the full Q10 curve; when it's tight, the curve bends flat. This is why the "effective Q10" you would measure from model output is lower than the parameter in hot, dry, or leafless periods.

5. A wrinkle worth knowing about (checked against the upstream code)

Several DALEC versions carry a sanity check ("EDC 4": fine roots must turn over faster than soil organic matter at the site's mean temperature). The code for it is t_root < t_som · Q10(Tmean/10) with Tmean in °C — i.e. a temperature multiplier referenced to 0 °C. That matched the original 2015 model, whose rate factor was eΘ·T. But every model since DALEC_811 evaluates the multiplier relative to the site mean (so it equals 1 at Tmean), and DALEC_1100 references it to 25 °C. The check was never updated to follow.

Does it matter for DALEC_1100? No — but for an unexpected reason. In DALEC_1100 the file containing this check (EDC1_1100.c) is never called: MODEL_INFO_1100.c:37 has the hook commented out, and 1100 uses a newer generic EDC list that has no root-vs-SOM temperature test at all. The line is dead code there. Consistent with that, 3.5% of accepted NL-Loo posterior draws violate it.

Where it is live: DALEC_1010–1012 and 1101–1105, 1110, whose models use the mean-referenced form Q10^((T − T_mean)/10). There the check as written is stricter than its stated intent by a factor Q10Tmean/10 — about 2× at a 10 °C site with Q10 = 2, about 5× at 25 °C — rejecting parameter sets in which roots do turn over faster than SOM. (EDC1_1101.c:64 and siblings; present on upstream main.)

6. Summary

  • All respiration temperature responses in DALEC_1100 are Q10 curves referenced to 25 °C, with priors of 1–5.
  • Plant fluxes use air temperature; soil fluxes use layer-1 soil temperature times the liquid fraction.
  • Plant respiration is additionally capped by carbon supply, so its effective temperature sensitivity is state-dependent.
  • Growth respiration has no temperature dependence of its own.
  • The older DALEC990 used one anomaly-referenced exponential for soil only, and a fixed fraction of GPP for plant respiration — no temperature term at all.
